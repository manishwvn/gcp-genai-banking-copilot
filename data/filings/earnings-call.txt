ACME FINANCIAL HOLDINGS, INC.
Q4 2025 Earnings Call Transcript
February 3, 2026

Operator: Good morning, and welcome to the Acme Financial Holdings fourth quarter 2025 earnings conference call. At this time all participants are in a listen-only mode. I will now turn the call over to the Chief Executive Officer.

CEO: Thank you, and good morning everyone. We are pleased to report another strong quarter, capping off a solid year for Acme Financial. Net income for the fourth quarter was $89.4 million, or $1.12 per diluted share, compared to $81.2 million, or $1.01 per diluted share, in the fourth quarter of last year.

Full year net income came in at $342.6 million, up 7.7% from the prior year. Our net interest margin expanded to 3.68% for the quarter, up from 3.52% a year ago, as we continued to benefit from disciplined deposit pricing and the repricing of our securities portfolio.

Loan growth remained measured but steady. Total loans grew 3.2% year over year, driven primarily by commercial and industrial lending. We continue to take a cautious approach to commercial real estate, particularly office properties, given the ongoing softness in that segment.

CFO: Turning to credit quality, our provision for credit losses was $11.8 million for the quarter, down from $13.4 million in the prior quarter. Net charge-offs remained low at 0.18% of average loans. Our allowance for credit losses stood at 1.24% of total loans at quarter end, which we believe is appropriately conservative given the current economic environment.

On the funding side, total deposits grew to $28.4 billion, up from $27.8 billion at the end of the third quarter. We saw particular strength in commercial deposit relationships, which grew 5.1% sequentially. Our loan to deposit ratio remains healthy at 78%, giving us ample capacity to support future loan growth.

Our common equity tier 1 ratio ended the year at 11.4%, and we returned $62 million to shareholders during the quarter through dividends and share repurchases.

Analyst (Jane Doe, Midwest Securities): Can you speak to your outlook for net interest margin in 2026, given expectations for Fed rate cuts?

CFO: Sure, Jane. We expect modest margin expansion in the first half of 2026 as deposit costs continue to reprice lower, even in a declining rate environment, given the lag in our deposit book. We are modeling two to three rate cuts in 2026 and believe our balance sheet is well positioned either way, given our mix of fixed and floating rate assets.

Analyst (Tom Nguyen, Capital Partners): What are you seeing in terms of loan demand from small business customers heading into 2026?

CEO: Small business loan demand has been resilient. We are seeing continued appetite for equipment financing and working capital lines, particularly in the healthcare and professional services sectors. We remain optimistic about mid-single-digit loan growth for the full year 2026.

Operator: This concludes the question and answer session. Thank you for joining us today.
